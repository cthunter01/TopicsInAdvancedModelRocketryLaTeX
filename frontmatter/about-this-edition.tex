\chapter{About This Edition}\label{front:about}

This is a newly typeset edition of \emph{Topics in Advanced Model Rocketry}, by Gordon K. Mandell,
George J. Caporaso and William P. Bengen, first published by The MIT Press in 1973. It is made from
the scan of the book that was released in 2025 under the title \emph{Topics in Advanced Model
Rocketry: T + 52 Years} and that, as its title page says, was digitized by Christopher L.
Feyerchak. The 1973 work was copyrighted by The Massachusetts Institute of Technology, which
transferred all rights to the authors in 1986; in 2025 the authors relinquished the copyright and
dedicated the work to the public domain, as the notice that follows the title page states. The
scan is a single file of 712 pages, \texttt{Topics\_in\_Advanced\_Model\_Rocketry.pdf}, with the
SHA-256 checksum
\begin{center}
  \small\texttt{51b26ed677ca17e7db7638ecc3454bbf77c8eedf78b19b3415b49dd7430c60ae}
\end{center}
This is the first version of the edition, prepared in 2026.

\section*{Contents of This Volume}

The volume follows the order of the scan. It opens with the front matter of 2025: the half-title,
the title page and the public-domain notice, a historical perspective and photographs of the
authors. The front matter of the 1973 edition follows, then this note and a table of contents made
for this edition, which takes the place of the 1973 one; the blank pages of the scan are left out.
Next comes the 1973 book itself: Chapter~\ref{ch1}, ``An Introduction to the Dynamics of Model
Rocket Flight'', by Caporaso and Mandell; Chapter~\ref{ch2}, ``A Unified Approach to Aerodynamic
Stability'', by Mandell; Chapter~\ref{ch3}, ``The Aerodynamic Drag of Model Rockets'', by Bengen;
Chapter~\ref{ch4}, ``Elements of Trajectory Analysis'', by Caporaso; then the four appendixes and
the figure credits.

The last Part, \hyperref[supp]{\emph{Errata and Supplement}}, reproduces the errata sheet issued
with the 1973 edition and the corrections and additions that the authors wrote later. Its
typewritten pages are transcribed, and its handwritten pages and displays are set in type. Only
some of these documents are signed and dated: Gordon Mandell's changes to Chapters~\ref{ch1}
and~\ref{ch4} (June 1994), his note on the notation of the pressure term (1 June 1994) and his
corrections to Chapter~\ref{ch2} of 15 February 2022. The others show no author or date. Among
them are the correction to page 40 of Chapter~\ref{ch1} and the other corrections to
Chapter~\ref{ch2}, typewritten and handwritten, which the scan places after the 1994 changes to
Chapter~\ref{ch1}. The editor's notes cite these by their titles.

\section*{How the Text Was Produced}

The text was transcribed from the page images of the scan. The mathematics, most of which the 1973
book letters by hand, was read symbol by symbol, magnified wherever a subscript or a Greek letter
was in doubt, and set in type. Each part of the transcription, and each correction applied to it,
was then checked against the scan in separate audit passes, which compared the typeset pages with
the page images. The audit reports, and the correction lists named below, are kept with the
edition's source files.

\section*{Conventions}

The wording, spelling, punctuation, units and paragraphing are those of the authors. Obvious typing
slips are corrected without comment. Among them is the one correction of the errata sheet that falls
outside the chapters, ``rockey'' for ``rocket'' in the 1973 Preface. A few slips of the
same kind in formulas and units are also corrected without comment: a parenthesis that is never
closed, ``Kg'' for ``kg'', and a symbol whose capital or small letter differs, for the same
quantity, from the form the chapter's Symbols list gives. Words that the typescript underlines, for
emphasis or in a title, are set in italics. Sections keep the book's numbers (``1.'', ``2.1'',
``3.1.2''). The book is laid out one-sided, for reading on a screen, and most cross-references are
links.

The running heads and the page numbers of the 1973 edition are not reproduced, and its page breaks
are not marked; only the correction pages for Chapter~\ref{ch3} in the last Part keep the page
numbers printed on them. Where an editor's note, or one of the documents in the last Part, cites a
page, it is a page of the 1973 edition unless the note says otherwise. Such a page cannot be looked
up directly in this edition; the outline under ``Corrections Applied'' below gives the section,
equation or figure that each correction concerns.

Equations are numbered at the right margin (the 1973 book numbers them at the left), in one series
per chapter and without a chapter prefix, as in the 1973 book; figures and tables are numbered in
the same way. Equations that a correction inserts between existing ones carry the authors' numbers:
\eqref{ch1:eq:n2a} in Chapter~\ref{ch1}, \eqref{ch3:eq:n144A} and \eqref{ch3:eq:n144B} in
Chapter~\ref{ch3}, \eqref{ch4:eq:n7a} to \eqref{ch4:eq:n7d} in Chapter~\ref{ch4}. Where the authors
renumber, their numbering is followed: after the correction to page 40 of Chapter~\ref{ch1}, the
equations that the 1973 edition numbers (21) and (22) are \eqref{ch1:eq:21} and \eqref{ch1:eq:22}.
Where a correction gives a new equation a number that an unchanged 1973 equation keeps, the new
equation is marked with a prime: \eqref{ch2:eq:n89} to \eqref{ch2:eq:n92} in Chapter~\ref{ch2},
\eqref{ch3:eq:n155} and \eqref{ch3:eq:n156} in Chapter~\ref{ch3}. A misprinted equation number that
no source corrects is kept as printed and explained in a note: in Chapter~\ref{ch4} the third member
of the group (154) is numbered \eqref{ch4:eq:156c}, where (154c) is meant.

\section*{Notation}

The typewriter and the hand lettering of the 1973 book often do not show whether a letter is a
capital (x or X, z or Z), or whether a small circle is the letter o or the digit 0. In each chapter
such a symbol is set in one form throughout. As a rule it is the form that the chapter's own
Symbols list gives, and because the lists differ, so do the chapters. Two conventions of this
edition take precedence: every o subscript of Chapter~\ref{ch1} is set as a zero, and so is the
subscript of the zero-lift drag coefficient $\CDo$ wherever it occurs.
\begin{itemize}
  \item In Chapter~\ref{ch1} the o subscripts, which the typewriter types as a small letter o, are
    set as zeros: $m_0$, $g_0$, $R_0$, $\CDo$.
  \item In Chapter~\ref{ch2} the axis subscripts of the angles and angular velocities are capitals
    ($\alpha_X$, $\Omega_Y$, and $\omega_Z$ for the roll rate), and initial values carry a zero
    ($\alpha_{X0}$, $\Omega_{Y0}$). Subscripts that the book types, rather than letters by hand,
    keep their printed form, as in $f_x(t)$ and $I_{Lo}$. The amplitude ratio, which the book
    writes as two separate letters, is set $\mathit{AR}$, and the aspect ratio as the joined
    $\AR$.
  \item In Chapter~\ref{ch3} the zero-lift drag coefficient $\CDo$ (with $(\CDo)_B$, $(\CDo)_F$
    and $(\CDo)_{FB}$) is the only symbol with a zero subscript; every other o subscript is the
    letter o ($x_o$, $p_o$, $\rho_o$, $\tau_o$). The roll rate is $\omega_Z$, as in
    Chapter~\ref{ch2}.
  \item In Chapter~\ref{ch4}, as its Symbols list prints them, the axis subscripts are lowercase
    ($\alpha_x$, $\omega_y$, and $\omega_z$ for the roll rate) and every o subscript is the letter
    o ($t_o$, $m_o$, $\alpha_{xo}$); only the $(\CDo)_{FB}$ of Table~\ref{ch4:tab:1} has a zero.
\end{itemize}
The initial mass, which the Symbols lists of Chapters~\ref{ch1} and~\ref{ch4} type alike, is thus
$m_0$ in Chapter~\ref{ch1} and $m_o$ in Chapter~\ref{ch4}, and the roll rate is $\omega_Z$ in
Chapters~\ref{ch2} and~\ref{ch3} and $\omega_z$ in Chapter~\ref{ch4}. These differences are
deliberate.

\section*{Editor's Notes}

Editorial notes are footnotes that begin ``\emph{Editor's note:}'' and are numbered E1, E2,
\ldots{} afresh in each chapter, separately from the authors' own footnotes. In figure captions, in
some table entries and in the last Part, a note is set in square brackets instead:
[\emph{Editor's note:} \ldots]. Most notes are of three kinds; the others explain a point of
notation or refer the reader to a related place in the book.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Corrections.} Where the errata sheet or one of the authors' later documents changes
    the text, the change is made, and a note says so and quotes what the 1973 edition read: every
    replaced equation in full, and the replaced prose in full or, for a long passage, the sentences
    whose content changed. Corrections that are purely typographical are made without a note.
  \item \emph{Doubts.} Where a formula or a statement appears to be wrong and no source corrects
    it, it is kept as printed, and a note explains what is evidently meant and why. Citations of
    equations, figures and sections that point to the wrong place are treated in the same way.
    Minor numerical discrepancies that change no conclusion (last digits, roundings, and a few
    small mismatches between the text and a figure) are left as printed without a note; they are
    recorded in the correction lists.
  \item \emph{Damage.} A few characters cut off at the edge of a scanned page are restored, and a
    note says from what.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Corrections Applied}

The corrections of the errata sheet and of the authors' later documents are applied in the
chapters, each at its place, with the notes described above. In outline:
\begin{description}
  \item[Chapter~\ref{ch1}.] Mandell's changes of June 1994 add the pressure term to the thrust
    equation~\eqref{ch1:eq:1} in Section~\ref{ch1:sec:2.1} and to its scalar, fore-and-aft
    form~\eqref{ch1:eq:2}. In~\eqref{ch1:eq:1} the term is written $(P_e - P_a)\vec{A}_e$, with the
    arrow over $A_e$ only, as his note of 1 June 1994 directs; the 1994 pages themselves, reproduced
    in the last Part, draw the arrow over the whole term. The changes extend the text around these
    equations with the exhaust and ambient pressures and a paragraph on over- and underexpansion. They add
    equation~\eqref{ch1:eq:n2a}, $\mdot = F/c$; replace Figure~\ref{ch1:fig:2} and its caption;
    define the specific impulse per unit weight of propellant, with new equations~\eqref{ch1:eq:5}
    to \eqref{ch1:eq:7} that relate it to the effective exhaust velocity $c_{\mathrm{eff}}$; and add
    five symbols to the Symbols list. The correction to page 40, which shows no author or date,
    rewrites the normal force equation~\eqref{ch1:eq:20} of Section~\ref{ch1:sec:2.3} in terms of
    the normal force coefficient $C_N$ and adds equations~\eqref{ch1:eq:n21} and
    \eqref{ch1:eq:n22}, which introduce the normal force curve slope $\CNa$; as it directs, the
    equations that follow are renumbered, so that the 1973 (21) and (22) are now
    \eqref{ch1:eq:21} and \eqref{ch1:eq:22}. The errata sheet corrects a symbol in the Symbols
    list.
  \item[Chapter~\ref{ch2}.] The errata sheet adds the term $M_s/C_1$ to
    equation~\eqref{ch2:eq:30} (Section~\ref{ch2:sec:3.1.2}), adds primes to $A$, $B$ and $C$ in
    four equations of Section~\ref{ch2:sec:3.2.1}, and supplies a missing $\alpha$ in
    the caption of Figure~\ref{ch2:fig:13}. The undated corrections to the chapter rewrite the
    introduction to the Barrowman method at the start of Section~\ref{ch2:sec:4.1}, which now
    defines $\CNa$ as the \emph{normal force curve slope}, the slope of the normal force coefficient
    $C_N$ against angle of attack. In the rest of that section and in the captions of
    Figures~\ref{ch2:fig:34} to~\ref{ch2:fig:36}, ``normal force coefficient'' becomes ``normal
    force curve slope'', except in the paragraph on body tube sections and in the section heading.

    Figure~\ref{ch2:fig:36} is replaced by the drawing that accompanies these corrections.
    Mandell's correction of 15 February 2022 replaces the passages of Section~\ref{ch2:sec:6.3}

  \item[Chapter~\ref{ch3}.] The errata sheet corrects two sentences, one in which a pressure change
    was reversed (Section~\ref{ch3:sec:4.2}) and one that gave the wrong direction in a diagram

\section*{What Is Not Applied}

A few instructions in the correction documents are not carried out, and the notes at the places
concerned say so.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Besides its corrections, the Summary for Chapter~\ref{ch4} plans new material, for which it
    gives equations and directions but no finished text: moving the notation and the discussion of
    the physics of two-stage flight from Section~\ref{ch4:sec:2.2.1} up to
    Section~\ref{ch4:sec:2.2}; revising the discussion of limiting behavior; moving the
    generalization to three or more stages; a new Section 2.2.3 on the features and limiting
    behavior of multistage solutions and on terminal velocity; a new Section 2.2.4 on upper stages
    whose thrust is equal to or less than their weight; and a treatment of the coasting phase by
    the Caporaso-Bengen method. None of this is done; the equations it gives for these plans can
    be read in the last Part. Apart from the corrected equations, four entries added to the list of
    notation and one sentence about an upper limit of integration, the 1973 text of
    Section~\ref{ch4:sec:2.2} is kept, including a discussion that the Summary calls unnecessary
    after its rewrite and a few sentences that no longer match the corrected equations; the notes
    point these out.
  \item The correction of Table~\ref{ch4:tab:1} states that the altitudes attained by rockets with
    canted fins change as a consequence. Those changes are not supplied: the results of
    Table~\ref{ch4:tab:2} for rolling rockets are those of the 1973 edition, computed with the 1973
    values, and are not recomputed.
  \item The undated handwritten correction to equations (115) to (117) of Chapter~\ref{ch2} is
    superseded by Mandell's correction of 15 February 2022 to the same passage and is not applied;
    it is reproduced in the last Part.
  \item No correction updates the Symbols lists of Chapters~\ref{ch1} and~\ref{ch2} for the normal
    force curve slope, so their entries keep the 1973 wording, with notes. Likewise the sentence of
    Chapter~\ref{ch2} that limits fin sets to three or four fins, which the new fin equations
    extend to six, is unchanged, with a note.
\end{itemize}
The 1973 forms of the replaced figures, Figure~\ref{ch1:fig:2} of Chapter~\ref{ch1},
Figure~\ref{ch2:fig:36} of Chapter~\ref{ch2} and Figure~\ref{ch4:fig:4} of Chapter~\ref{ch4}, are
reproduced in the last Part beside their replacements.

\section*{Figures}

In this first version of the edition the figures and the photographic plates are reproduced from
the scan, each cropped from its page; the replacement figures are cropped from the supplement pages,
and Figure~\ref{ch2:fig:52} of Chapter~\ref{ch2} is the chart that accompanies the 2022 correction.
The tables and the Symbols lists are typeset. Many figures carry formulas or tables in the artwork
itself, and these stay part of the images: a list kept for a later version names 31 such figures in
Chapter~\ref{ch2} alone, from the formulas beneath the flywheel of Figure~\ref{ch2:fig:5} to the
table of measurements in Figure~\ref{ch2:fig:46}, and Figures~\ref{ch3:fig:29}, \ref{ch3:fig:31},
\ref{ch3:fig:39}b, \ref{ch3:fig:44} and~\ref{ch3:fig:45} of Chapter~\ref{ch3} contain tables drawn
in the artwork. A later version may redraw the figures and set their lettering in type.
